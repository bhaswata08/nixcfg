# Words

Adapted from the `stop-slop` skill by Hardik Pandya, MIT licensed, trimmed for
slides. Its full phrase and structure lists are worth reading for long prose.
On a slide you have one sentence, so most of its rhythm advice does not apply.
The bans do.

## Punctuation

- **No em dashes and no en dashes.** Anywhere. They are the loudest AI tell in
  the language. A comma, a full stop, or two sentences.
- **No exclamation marks.**
- **No ellipses** used for drama.
- Straight quotes, not curly, so they render the same everywhere.

## Openers to delete

Delete the first clause and see if the sentence got worse. Usually it got
better.

- "It is worth noting that", "It is important to understand that"
- "In today's fast-paced world", "In the era of"
- "Let's dive into", "Let's explore", "Let's take a look at"
- "At its core", "Fundamentally", "Essentially", "Ultimately"
- "As we can see", "As mentioned above"

## Phrases to delete

- "leverage" for use, "utilize" for use
- "seamless", "robust", "powerful", "cutting-edge", "state of the art",
  "game-changing", "revolutionary", "unlock", "empower", "harness"
- "a wide range of", "a variety of", "a plethora of"
- "delve into", "navigate the landscape", "at scale" as decoration
- "not only X but also Y"
- "it's not just X, it's Y"

## Structures to avoid

- **The rule of three where two would do.** "Fast, cheap and reliable" when only
  two of those were measured.
- **The binary contrast.** "This is not about X. It is about Y." It sounds
  decisive and carries no information.
- **The rhetorical question as a heading.** "But what does this actually cost?"
  Just say what it costs.
- **Hedged claims.** "may potentially help improve" is four words of nothing.
  Either it did or it did not, and if you do not know, say you do not know.
- **Inanimate subjects doing human things.** "The architecture decides", "the
  data tells us". A team decided. A measurement showed.

## Positively

**Be specific.** "Roughly 80 small runs to choose the math data" beats
"extensive experimentation". Specificity is the entire value of a technical
talk, and it is what separates a deck from a blog post.

**Active voice with a real subject.** "They reported wall clock, not dollars"
beats "wall clock time was reported".

**Trust the audience.** Say the thing once. Do not explain the joke, restate the
heading, or announce what you are about to say.

**Simple English.** Short words. If a plain word and a technical word mean the
same thing, use the plain one. Keep the technical word when it is the actual
term of art, and then do not apologise for it.

## The heading test

A heading is the most-read text on the slide. Give it the claim.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Timeline | Where the 56 days went |
| Data Considerations | Throwing out the bottom 40% is affordable |
| Systems Overview | 512 GB before a single activation |
| Conclusion | The interesting question is no longer how |

## Two checks before you ship

1. **Directness.** Does every sentence say something that could be false? A
   sentence that cannot be wrong is not carrying information.
2. **Density.** Delete every word that can go. Then read it again. On a slide
   this usually converges at about twelve words.
